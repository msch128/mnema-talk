# Fix review: H-L8-07 (unread SRTCP streams of a member's media connection)

Reviewer: phase 3, read-only. I wrote neither the finding nor the fix. Branch msch128/fix-h-l8-07, commit c3ee1762b
(on 39286b819). Temporary worktrees of c3ee1762b and of 39286b819 (the base, for comparisons) were removed
afterwards. Probe test files lived only inside them. Nothing was committed or pushed. Everything ran on loopback, with
no audio device, camera or microphone. Load was 4 to 9 for all runs, `nice -n 10`, `go test -p 1`, one job at a time.

Verdict: **MERGE AFTER FIXES.** The fix bounds the memory for every attack vector I tried. One honest-client risk
must be settled before merge (BLOCKER-pending 1), and SECURITY.md overstates one bound (SHOULD-FIX 2).

---

## What the fix does (as read)

- Every member connection gets its own Pion API (`connAPI`, sfu_setup.go:135-168). The settings struct is copied,
  and the media engine and interceptor registry are shared as before. The API carries:
  - a logger factory, `rtcpStreamCap`, which wraps only the "pc" scope;
  - a buffer factory that limits SRTCP buffers to 128 packets and 16 KB (RTP buffers stay at 1 MB).
- `rtcpCountingLogger.Warnf` matches Pion's format string "Incoming unhandled RTCP ssrc(%d), OnTrack will not be
  fired" exactly and counts each stream Pion keeps.
- SSRCs that `addSender` recorded (sender plus RTX) are "forgiven": they count only against the total cap.
- Past 64 unknown streams, or 2048 in all, the connection is closed on its own goroutine. The close logs once at warn,
  counts in `mnema_sfu_ingress_dropped_total{reason="rtcp_ssrcs"}`, and from then on every SRTCP stream gets a closed
  zero-size buffer.

I checked the Pion sources the fix relies on (webrtc v4.2.26, srtp v3.1.3, logging v0.2.4):
- peerconnection.go:2327-2351: the processor goroutine logs the line once per accepted stream with `ssrc` as a
  `uint32`, which matches the type assertion in ingress.go.
- api.go:40-41 and peerconnection.go:143: the "pc" logger comes from the API's LoggerFactory.
- srtp stream_srtcp.go:116-136: the BufferFactory is called on every stream init. The default buffer was a
  `packetBuffer` limited to 100 KB, which `attrBuffer` reproduces (attributes pass through
  `ReadWithAttributes`/`WriteWithAttributes`).
- session_srtcp.go:241-251: a write error from the closed buffer ends that compound packet. This happens only after
  the trip.
- srtp session.go:163: the read loop logs decrypt errors and goes on.

## 1. Does it bound memory?

My own flood vectors, unthrottled, each on a fresh member connection, 900 to 3000 distinct SSRCs per vector:

| Vector | SSRCs named before the connection stopped writing | Streams Pion kept (all unknown) | Closed |
|---|---|---|---|
| SR, 31 blocks | 1333 | 83 | yes |
| BYE, 31 sources (after an RR) | 3007 | 86 | yes |
| SDES, 31 chunks | 1116 | 80 | yes |
| REMB, 100 SSRCs | 3000 | 90 | yes |
| FIR, 30 entries | 1470 | 84 | yes |
| NACK, 40 per compound | 1480 | 82 | yes |
| XR DLRR, 30 | 900 | 78 | yes |

The author's test adds PLI and 31-block RRs; both are covered too.

**Heap per connection.** Heap figures are after two forced GCs. While the connection is still open:
- At the cap with filled streams: my slow drip (one new SSRC every 50 ms, 30 reports each) stood at +1.20 MB with 63
  streams. The author's filled test measured +1.19 MB for 62 streams (19.2 KB per stream). Without the fix such a
  stream costs about 113 KB with 200 packets and about 320 KB when full.
- After the close, the memory is freed within about a second: the heap one second later was already at or below the
  baseline in every vector.
- A slow drip below any rate does not escape the cap, because the cap counts streams, not rate. It closed at 65.

**Race between the trip and the close.** Four goroutines flooding PLIs unthrottled, three rounds: Pion made 0, 5 and 7
streams after the trip, each with no buffer. The `closing` flag is atomic and checked by the buffer factory, the
pointer to the connection is atomic, the counters are under a leaf mutex, and `-race` over the package is clean.

**The forgiven set.** Can an attacker ride it to 2048 × 20 KB? Yes, and faster than the commit message and SECURITY.md
say. My probe: a member toggled its subscription to one other member's screen share through `Room.Subscribe`.
- Each "on" adds a new sender with two SSRCs (media and RTX). The hub's `webrtc_subscribe` has no rate limit (the
  voice gate covers joins and leaves only).
- 1100 toggles took 12.9 s and recorded 1101 SSRCs. Filling their streams with 31-block RRs cost +20.9 MB
  (18,975 B per stream), and the connection stayed open.
- With 2200 toggles (27 s), the total cap tripped at 2049 streams and closed the connection, as designed.

So one member can hold about 2048 × 19 KB ≈ 39 MB indefinitely by stopping at 2047. Reaching it needs only one
publisher in the room and about half a minute; it does not take "2048 tracks nobody reaches". How many members does it
take to exhaust the server? With the shipped 1 GB container limit (GOMEMLIMIT about 870 MB) and a baseline of a
few hundred MB, it takes roughly 15 to 22 cooperating members, each holding its own connection; one member alone
cannot. That is a bounded, multi-member cost, so I do not call it a blocker. But the documented bound is wrong
(SHOULD-FIX 2), and it is cheap to shrink (see there).

## 2. The logger hook

- **Log level.** Pion filters by level inside `DefaultLeveledLogger.logf` (logging logger.go:149-151). The wrapper sees
  each `Warnf` call before that filter, so the default Error level and any `PION_LOG_*` setting make no difference.
  The app's LOG_LEVEL does not touch Pion: the only other Pion logger factory in the repo is the TURN relay's
  (internal/turnrelay/server.go:85). Every member connection gets its logger factory from `connAPI`. The shared
  `s.api` (connection test: data channel only, no SRTP) has no cap and needs none.
- **Pion changes the text.** I changed the matched text in `pionUnhandledRTCP` (`ssrc` -> `SSRC`):
  TestUnhandledSRTCPStreamsAreCapped FAILS ("Pion kept 0 streams (0 unknown), heap +4038656 bytes ... not capped").
  A Pion bump that changes the line therefore turns CI red. Two caveats:
  - TestRTCPStreamCapForgivesWhatTheSFUSent still passes then, because it calls `unhandled` directly.
  - In production the cap would silently stop working while each stream keeps the smaller buffer (about 20 KB filled,
    against 2.7 KB unfilled per stream).
- **A sturdier signal.** Now that the API is per connection, the buffer factory itself is a per-connection hook that
  Pion calls for every SRTCP stream, with its SSRC. It cannot tell a read stream from an unread one on its own, but
  it can carry a backstop that does not depend on Pion's wording: a cap on the total SRTCP buffers it has handed out.
  The string remains the precise signal, so it is still needed for the 64. NOTE 3: add the backstop.

## 3. One API per connection

- **What the shared API shared, and what is shared now.**
  - The SettingEngine is copied by value, as `WithSettingEngine` always did. Its pointers are copied with it, so the
    one ICE UDP mux, the port range, the announced addresses and the network types stay single.
  - The MediaEngine pointer is shared; Pion copies it per PeerConnection anyway (the fix does not set
    `disableMediaEngineCopy`).
  - The interceptor registry is shared. It holds the bandwidth estimator factory, NACK, TWCC, telemetry and the
    viewerGuard, and Pion builds its interceptors per connection, as before.
  - The logger factory is one `DefaultLoggerFactory` from the builder, as NewAPI would have picked.
  - NewAPI with these options allocates only its own struct and an "api" logger.
  - Nothing that must be single is duplicated.
- **Join cost.** 100 `s.Join` calls into one room, with probe test files on both commits:

  | Commit | Time per join | Mallocs per join | Bytes allocated per join | Heap kept per peer |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | base 39286b819 | 247 µs | 10,736 | 1,059,224 | 399 KB |
  | fix c3ee1762b | 278 µs | 10,739 | 1,056,461 | 412 KB |

  The differences are within noise.
- **Forwarding path.** `make bench-allocs` shows 0 allocs/op on all four forward benchmarks.
- **Cycle tests.** With SFU_CYCLE_ROUNDS=400, each run three times per commit:

  - TestSFURoomOpenCloseCycleReturnsToBaseline: base +11.9 / 8.7 / 8.9 MB, fix +9.1 / 9.0 / 9.5 MB.
  - Goroutines are at baseline, except one transient extra goroutine in one fix run.

## 4. Honest clients

What creates an unknown stream: RTCP from the client whose destination SSRC (a report block, a feedback media SSRC,
an SDES chunk, a BYE source) is something the SFU neither reads nor has sent on that connection.

- **The client's own published SSRCs.** The web client starts and stops its camera, screen and screen audio with
  `replaceTrack` on stable transceivers (camera.ts:42/90, screen.ts:69/232, state.ts:54 "never renegotiates"). The
  SSRCs do not change, and the SFU keeps reading a stopped track (stoppedAt), so stopping and starting a share many
  times creates no unknown stream. Simulcast layers could get an SR before Pion's RID probe opens the stream: at most
  one per layer SSRC per connection (about 12 for camera and screen with RTX), once each.
- **Chrome, Safari, Electron.** libwebrtc gives its receive streams one shared RTCP SSRC. My probe with a Chrome-like
  viewer of 70 remote audio lines (RR plus SDES with one shared SSRC, three rounds) gave 1 unknown stream, and the
  connection stayed open.
- **Firefox: BLOCKER-pending 1.** Firefox signals an `a=ssrc` on each recvonly m-line and uses it as the sender SSRC of
  that line's receiver reports ([Mozilla bug 1397756](https://bugzilla.mozilla.org/show_bug.cgi?id=1397756);
  [bug 1335469](https://bugzilla.mozilla.org/show_bug.cgi?id=1335469) is related). If it also sends the SDES CNAME
  chunk of the compound packet with that SSRC, as compound RTCP normally does, every line the SFU sends to a Firefox
  viewer adds one unknown stream. Pion does not read the SSRC of a line the remote only receives on.

  My probe with a Firefox-like viewer of 70 remote audio lines (RR plus SDES with one SSRC per line) gave 72 unknown
  streams, and the cap closed the honest viewer's connection.

  A Firefox member in a Talk with more than about 60 receive lines (49 others' microphones plus visible cameras and a
  share) would lose all media silently: the client shows one "no media connection" toast and does not rejoin (see 5).
  The author's instrumented suite cannot see this, because Pion clients send no per-line SDES.

  I could not confirm Firefox's SDES behaviour without a browser (none may run here). Settle it before merge, with
  either of:
  - a Firefox call spec in CI (they exist: ci.yml:950-973) that checks the server's count of unknown streams after a
    two-member call. 0 clears this point; 1 or more per received line confirms it;
  - or, independent of the outcome, forgive SSRCs that the remote description declares (the `a=ssrc` lines of the
    client's answer), read lazily on the unknown path only. That is cheap and also covers any browser that signals
    per-line SSRCs.
- **Long sessions and the 2048.** An honest viewer adds forgiven streams for senders the SFU removed while late
  reports were still on the way:
  - The author measured 18 for 41 departures (about 0.44 each).
  - The web client also unsubscribes cameras whose tiles leave the screen (cameraVisibility.ts, 800 ms delay). Each
    scroll removes senders, and each removal is worth up to 2 forgiven SSRCs.

  At about 0.5 per removal, an honest connection reaches 2048 after roughly 4000 removals (departures plus off-screen
  cameras), which is rare but possible in a long, large Talk. NOTE 4.

## 5. The close

The hub is not told: the member keeps a silent seat, as with the existing ingress-abuse close. The web client reacts to
ICE "failed" only with a one-time toast (peer.ts:88-92, mediaRoom.ts:99-103). It rejoins only after a WebSocket
reconnect (session.ts:177-181), so the member stays without media until they leave and join again by hand.

For an attacker this is fine, and consistent with the existing ingress-abuse close. For a false positive (point 4)
it is the reason the honest bound matters.

**Reconnect and flood.** Each round costs one join (the voice gate allows 1 per second, burst 3) and holds at most
about 1.2 MB of streams plus about 0.4 MB of connection until the close, which frees within about a second. One member
looping keeps a few MB live at most. There is no back-off beyond the voice gate, and none is needed for memory.

## 6. Locks, goroutines, documentation

- **Locks.**
  - `unhandled` runs on Pion's processor goroutine, holds no SFU lock, takes only `c.mu` (a leaf) and starts the close
    on a new goroutine. The processor goroutine is the one the SRTCP session waits for while closing, so closing
    inline could hang.
  - `sent` reads `GetParameters` before taking `c.mu`. It is called from `addSender`, and nothing takes `c.mu` and
    then another lock.
  - The peer leaves the room through the existing closed-state handler.
  - I read the changed parts of sfu.go, sfu_join.go (Join, newReceiveConn), room_attach.go (attachPeer),
    room_signal.go (addSender, removeStaleSenders) and sfu_setup.go fully.
  - SetAnnouncedIPs replaces the builder under `apiMu`, and connAPI reads it under the read lock. Before `bind`, the
    connection pointer is nil and no close can trip; DTLS is not up yet, so no SRTCP can arrive then.
- **Revert check.** With the caps and the buffer factory disabled, all three new tests FAIL. With the fix, they pass.
- **SECURITY.md.** It is right that the cap counts SSRCs "the SFU never sent". The last sentence ("about 41 MB in the
  case nobody reaches (2048 tracks sent to it and stopped while it stays connected)") is wrong: subscription toggles
  reach it in about 27 s with one publisher (SHOULD-FIX 2).
- **doc/operations.md.** The `rtcp_ssrcs` row matches the code.
- **sim-sweep.yml.** Only the test name and the step name change; triggers, timeout and permissions are unchanged.
- **Commit.** The type `fix:` is right.

## Findings

- **BLOCKER-pending 1** (ingress.go:256-267, `unhandled`): if Firefox's per-recvonly-line RTCP SSRC (signalled as
  `a=ssrc` on that line) appears as an SDES chunk, an honest Firefox viewer of more than about 64 lines is
  disconnected, silently and without a rejoin. The mechanism is proven with a simulated client (72 unknown streams
  for 70 lines, connection closed); the Firefox behaviour is supported by Mozilla bug 1397756 but not measured.
  Settle it with a CI Firefox check, or forgive the SSRCs declared in the remote description.
- **SHOULD-FIX 2** (SECURITY.md new paragraph; commit message "Bound per connection"): the 2048 × 20 KB ≈ 39 to 41 MB
  per member is reachable by any member in about half a minute through `webrtc_subscribe` toggles; it is not "the
  case nobody reaches". Fix the text. Also consider handing a forgiven re-created stream (an SSRC whose sender's own
  stream was already given a buffer) a discarding buffer: Write returns len, nil; Read blocks until Close. Do not use
  the closed one, whose write error would cut the rest of an honest compound packet. That brings the forgiven
  streams to about 3 KB each (about 6 MB per member at 2048).
- **NOTE 3** (ingress.go:212-227): the cap depends on Pion's exact log text. A change is caught by
  TestUnhandledSRTCPStreamsAreCapped (verified), but nothing caps in production if it slips through. A count of SRTCP
  buffers handed out in the per-connection buffer factory would be a backstop independent of the text.
- **NOTE 4**: honest forgiven streams accumulate with departures and off-screen camera churn for the life of a
  connection (about 0.5 per removed sender); 2048 is far but finite for a long, large Talk. A false trip costs that
  member its media until a manual rejoin.

## Round 2: commit fd4645559 (on c3ee1762b)

Reviewed after `git fetch origin`, in a temporary worktree (removed). Probe test files lived only inside it. Load was
3 to 5, `nice -n 10`, `-p 1`, one job at a time.

Round 2 verdict: **MERGE AFTER FIXES.** Points 1, 3 and 4 of round 1 are closed. Point 2 is closed for the toggle ride.
But the new "forgive an SSRC once a later answer declares it" step lets a member move buffered streams out of the
unknown count. That raises the bound for arbitrary SSRCs from about 1.3 MB to about 20 MB per member, and SECURITY.md
states the lower figure (SHOULD-FIX R2-1). The bound still holds, so this is not a blocker.

### What changed (as read)

- `rtcpStreamCap` is now both the logger and the buffer factory of the connection (ingress.go:110-350).
- Per SRTCP stream, the factory gives a capped `rtcpBuffer` for an SSRC the SFU reads (the media SSRC of a track it
  sends now) or one nobody accounts for. It gives a `quietBuffer` for an SSRC the client declared, an RTX SSRC of a
  sent track, or one of the last 1024 retired SSRCs. A quietBuffer's Write takes the packet and keeps nothing; its
  Read waits until Close (transport.go).
- The connection is closed past 64 unknown SSRCs, 32,768 quiet streams, or 1024 buffered streams open at once. The
  last cap is counted by the factory, so it does not depend on Pion's log line.
- SetAnswer calls `declare` with the applied answer (at most 4 SSRCs per m-line; the set is replaced each time; any
  SSRC in it leaves the unknown set). `removeStaleSenders` calls `stoppedTrack`.

### Pion facts checked (webrtc v4.2.26, srtp v3.1.3)

- RTPSender opens an SRTCP read stream only for the media SSRC (srtp_writer_future.go:63). Its Close closes it
  (:85-98), which reaches `rtcpBuffer.Close`, so the buffered count goes down and the SSRC is retired.
- The SFU reads RTCP only from senders (room_tracks.go:24). No Pion goroutine reads a receiver's or an unhandled
  stream on its own.
- The srtp session's close does not close the read streams; their owners do: the processor's deferred loop for
  unhandled streams (peerconnection.go:2328-2333), and senders and receivers on Stop.

So the quiet buffer's Read blocks nobody in practice, and where a reader exists, Close releases it. Nothing leaks at
close. Goroutines were at baseline in the cycle tests, and `-race` is clean.

### Round 1 vectors again (unthrottled, fresh member connection each)

- PLI, RR(31), SR(31), BYE(31), SDES(31), REMB(100), FIR(30), NACK(40), XR DLRR(30), 3000 SSRCs each: every
  connection closed at 68 to 76 unknown streams. Buffered open fell back to 0 after the close.
- 62 filled unknown streams on an open connection: +1.10 MB.
- Trip race (4 goroutines flooding PLIs, 3 rounds): 66 unknown each, closed.
- Toggle ride: see below.

### Declaring around the cap (your first question)

- **How many m-lines.** Every m-line of the answer counts. That is the SFU's fixed lines (8 in a fresh room) plus
  one per extra track it sends (transceivers are reused), so the count is bounded by the room's tracks, not by the
  client.
- **More than one SSRC per line.** Adding more than one a=ssrc to a line the client sends on crashes the process
  through a Pion bug that is present on main, independent of this fix. That is a separate defect, reported to the
  coordinator privately, not detailed here. So in practice an attacker gets about 1 per line without crashing
  (worse: it can crash the server instead).
- **Rotation, measured.** Every answer declared fresh SSRCs on each of the 7 lines without a=ssrc, and the client
  named them all. Over 20 s: 788 answers, 5530 declared SSRCs named (276 per second), 5578 quiet streams, 14 unknown
  (reports that landed after the next answer had replaced the set), 15 buffered; the connection stayed open.

  At that rate the 32,768 quiet cap is reached in about 2 minutes, not "a quarter of an hour". In a room with many
  lines it is reached faster: up to 4 × lines per answer if Pion tolerated it. The bound is still 32,768 × about
  0.5 KB, about 16 MB, then the close.
- **Laundering buffered streams through declare (SHOULD-FIX R2-1).** `declare` drops from the unknown set every SSRC
  the new answer names, but the buffered stream Pion already made for it keeps its 16 KB buffer.

  Unit probe on the cap (no connection, so nothing closes): 40 rounds of 32 unknown streams (each given a buffer, then
  logged), each followed by an answer declaring them (8 lines × 4). Result: unknown 0, buffered 1280. With a
  connection, the backstop closes at 1025 buffered.

  So a member can hold about 1024 × 19 KB ≈ 20 MB of filled buffers for arbitrary SSRCs, not 1.3 MB. My end-to-end
  attempt did not reproduce the climb (buffered stayed at 63; I did not find out why), so this rests on the unit probe
  and on reading `declare`.

  The forgiveness is needed: Firefox's first report from a new line can arrive before its answer. It can be bounded,
  for example by forgiving a formerly unknown SSRC only once per m-line, or by counting forgiven-unknown streams
  against their own small cap. Then the 1.3 MB in SECURITY.md holds again.

### The 1024 retired window (your second question)

An SSRC falls out only after 1024 later retirements. The first late report after a stop creates the stream, and
later reports reuse it, so only that first report matters. It comes within one or two RTCP intervals, about 1 to 5 s.
Losing one would take more than 1024 senders removed from one viewer within those seconds. No honest Talk does that:
a whole 50-member room leaving at once is about 150 to 200 SSRCs. Count: 0 in any plausible case.

### Toggle cost (H-L8-05 path, the author's note)

- 400 subscription events against one publisher's share (one on or off each, 25 ms apart, 10.2 s), no reports:
  +6.58 MB, 16.4 KB per event (about 33 KB per sender created and removed). 28 quiet streams, connection open.
- The same with reports on every retired SSRC: +9.88 MB, 401 quiet streams. The difference of 3.3 MB is within the
  run-to-run noise of these heap numbers; the author's controlled test gives 409 to 546 B per quiet stream.
- Correction to the author's figure (10 KB per toggle, 20 toggles a second, 200 KB a second): each
  `webrtc_subscribe` event is one toggle. The ordinary budget lets 40 a second through (internal/ws/budget.go:13), so
  I measure about 16 KB × 40 ≈ 650 KB a second of retained heap per member, for the life of its connection.
- A camera batch event names up to 16 publishers (and `all: true` names every camera), so in a room with many
  cameras one event can stop up to 16 senders. By extrapolation, not measured: about 10 MB a second.

This is H-L8-05's retention driven on purpose by one member, unbounded until the connection ends. It is not this
fix's path, but it is now the larger member-driven growth, and I recommend recording it as a finding of its own
(S3, the same reasoning as H-L8-07).

## Round 3: commit e365c714d (on fd4645559)

A small delta check after `git fetch origin`, in a temporary worktree (removed); probe files lived only inside it. Load
was 4 to 5.

Round 3 verdict: **MERGE AFTER FIXES.** The laundering through repeated answers is closed, and the honest race is still
forgiven. But the m-line count the budget uses comes from the client's answer, and Pion accepts an answer with more
m-lines than the offer. The bound is therefore "65 + whatever m-lines fit in one 64 KB answer", about 9 MB rather
than "under 3 MB" (SHOULD-FIX R3-1). It is bounded below the 1024 backstop, so this is not a blocker.

### Checked
