ID: H-L8-01, lane 8, severity S4, status: reproduced (pending phase 2)

Claim:
The in-memory rate limiter and login-failure limiter evict an established,
not-currently-locked victim entry when flooded with fresh keys, resetting the
victim's count/window; only an *active lockout* is durable. The existing tests
assert the map size and that the newest key survives, nothing about the victim.

Property:
- Stated/intended: `internal/httpx/loginfailure.go` makeRoomLocked says it
  evicts "preferring entries that are not locked, so flooding the map with fresh
  keys does not cheaply lift a lockout", and the `login` docstring
  (`internal/auth/authn/handler.go`) describes escalating lockouts whose tier is
  "forgotten after an hour without failures" -- i.e. accumulated failures are
  meant to persist until they decay or lock. The account-wide guess cap
  (`accountFailures`, threshold 100, `handler.go:120`) is an unlocked, long-lived
  entry for its entire climb from 0 to 100.
- Broken: an unlocked entry below its threshold (e.g. accountFailures at 99/100)
  and an at-limit `RateLimiter` window are the *preferred* eviction victims
  (earliest reset / unlocked+stalest among a random sample of `evictSample`=32).
  A flood of fresh keys at the cap (`defaultMaxEntries`=65536) resets them. This
  lets a flooder keep a targeted account's failure count from ever reaching the
  lockout threshold, defeating the account-wide brute-force cap; and lets a
  party reset a per-IP window on secret-guarding endpoints.
- Durable half (now guarded): a *currently locked* entry is NOT evicted
  (TestLoginFailureLockedVictimSurvivesFlood passes at the full cap).

Repro (clean checkout of msch128/hunt-lane8, commit 0b742e657):
  go test ./internal/httpx/ -run 'Flood|StalestVictim' -v
  # deep, production cap 65536:
  RL_FLOOD_DEEP=1 go test ./internal/httpx/ -run 'Flood|StalestVictim' -v
Expected/actual:
- TestLoginFailureLockedVictimSurvivesFlood: PASS (locked victim durable).
- TestLoginFailurePartialVictimEvictedByFlood: 24/24 trials (3/3 deep) evict the
  99/100 victim; measured horizon ~cap/evictSample inserts (128 at cap 4096,
  2048 at cap 65536).
- TestRateLimiterStalestVictimEvictedByFlood: 24/24 (3/3 deep) evict the
  at-limit window; re-insertion yields a fresh budget.

Minimal:
At the cap, one fresh-key insert evicts one entry from a sample of 32 preferring
the unlocked/earliest-reset one; the stalest unlocked victim is reliably gone
after ~cap/32 inserts. `evictSampled` (internal/httpx/bounded.go:31).

Reach:
- accountFailures reset (lockout evasion): the flooder must hold 65536 distinct
  entries in that limiter. Each entry is created only by a failed login against
  a distinct username, which burns a bcrypt check (global `bcrypt_slots`, ~NumCPU
  concurrent with a 2s queue) and draws a `loginPerIP` slot (60/15min per IPv6
  /64). Expensive and distributed, but sustainable for a determined attacker
  with many /64s; not a casual attack. Anon (no account needed; usernames need
  only match the pattern, not exist).
- per-IP window reset: a party controlling a large IPv6 allocation (a /48 gives
  65536 /64 keys) can fill a per-IP limiter and steer eviction of an established
  window. For the attacker's own key this is moot (fresh /64s already give fresh
  budget); it matters only for resetting another party's established entry.

Effect:
The account-wide guess cap -- the control that slows targeted online password
guessing once an account is under attack -- can be prevented from ever firing,
so guessing is not throttled account-wide. Measured in-process; no production
timing measured (not shown).

Not shown:
- End-to-end reachability through the real HTTP handlers under the bcrypt-slot
  and per-IP throttles (measured only on the limiter data structures).
- Whether the per-IP reset is cheaper on any specific endpoint than simply using
  fresh /64s.

Fix (direction only):
Make eviction never prefer a victim that still holds protective state: evict the
youngest/idle entries, not the stalest established one; or make an established
non-zero count sticky (evict only entries at count 0 / fully-refilled windows),
falling back to refuse-rather-than-grow as the TURN limiter does. The locked-is-
durable guard (TestLoginFailureLockedVictimSurvivesFlood) already encodes the
intent; extend it to any non-zero established count.
