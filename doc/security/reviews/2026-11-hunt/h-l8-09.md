## Phase 2

### Reproduction

On `1c787fdc2`, `go test ./internal/sfu/ -run TestSubscriptionSwitches -v`
measured +3 175 bytes per switch over 1 000 switches. Goroutines +0, offer
26 984 → 26 984 bytes. The inverse assertion holds.

### Is it documented?

No. Before the fix, SECURITY.md (media connection limits) said nothing about
per-sender state kept for the life of a connection.

### Is it a duplicate of H-L8-03 or H-L8-05?

**H-L8-03.** At `ff88d92aa` (H-L8-03's fix merged, no cap yet), the same test
at 4 000 switches measures **+265 bytes per switch**, down from 3 175. So
about 92% of the measured retention is H-L8-03's defect: the stopped sender's
writer chain, pinned by the pacer. H-L8-09's headline figure is H-L8-03 seen
from the member's side.

H-L8-09 is still a finding of its own, for two reasons:

- **Reach.** H-L8-03 is rated S4: passive churn, "no attacker needed". H-L8-09
  is an ordinary member driving the same retention on purpose, at the speed of
  its own events.
- **What is left.** H-L8-03's fix does not touch the residual of a few hundred
  bytes per sender (Pion's per-SSRC state and the rest). Without the cap it is
  unbounded for the life of the connection and drivable by the member.

**H-L8-05** is a different path: state kept on a viewer's connection per
member who joined and left. Its known failure is unchanged on the fix tip:
25 470 bytes per joiner at 1 200 rounds, inverse assertion still holds.

### Severity

S3 stands. Ordinary members can bypass the per-connection bounds SECURITY.md
states, in both states:

- **Before any fix:** with camera batches of 16 (`SubscribeCameras`, the path
  of the batch event), I measured 6 843 bytes per sender. At the WebSocket
  budget of 40 events a second (20 off/on toggles, 320 senders a second) that
  is about **2.2 MB a second per member**.
- **With H-L8-03's fix only:** about 273 bytes per sender, both ends of the
  connection (measured on the tip below, which differs from the H-L8-03-only
  state by the cap alone). At the event budget that is about 87 KB a second
  per member, unbounded, about 1 GB in 3.4 hours, times the number of members.

Corrections to the claim:

- "16.4 KB per event" includes warm-up. The steady figure is about 3.2 KB per
  switch on a screen share, about 6.8 KB per sender.
- "About 10 MB a second" with camera batches is about 4.5 times too high.
  Measured and scaled to the event budget it is about 2.2 MB a second.

## Fix review (tip 714225dd4)

### What still grows, measured

I diffed heap profiles taken after two forced GCs (`MemProfileRate` 1024) and
fitted the heap in use per switch.

- **Screen share** (the fix's own test, 2 000 switches): +118 to +430 bytes per
  switch, depending on run and load (118 under `-race`). Goroutines +0, offer
  flat.
- **Cameras, not measured by the author:**
  - 4 publishers, 600 toggles, 2 400 senders: +836 bytes per sender, with the
    curve flattening.
  - 16 publishers (full batches), 300 toggles, 4 800 senders: **+273 bytes per
    sender** for both ends. That matches the author's 160 to 240 bytes on the
    server side.
- The profile diff on the server side shows no stopped sender and no writer
  chain. What remains is SDP and codec parse objects of the current
  descriptions (live in both snapshots, noise) and a small SRTP/SRTCP share.
- In rapid toggles most senders never encrypt a packet before they are
  stopped, so the SRTP state per sender is below the author's 156 bytes. Either
  way, the cost is a few hundred bytes per sender, and the cap bounds it.
- **Harness note:** in my first camera runs the publishers' own NACK send
  history filled up during the run (`rtpbuffer` copies under the test
  client's `publish` loop). That is test-client growth, not the SFU. Filling
  both tracks' histories first removes it. The fix's screen-share test already
  does this (`fillSendHistory`).

### Pace

Measured on the tip with batches of 16 (in process, no event budget) on this
machine, at a load of about 10: about 164 senders a second, limited by the
renegotiation round trip. Before the fix the same probe on a quieter machine
ran at about 970 a second. The author's figure, 320 senders a second at the
event budget, is the right upper bound to plan with. At that pace one member
reaches the cap in about 100 to 200 seconds.

### BLOCKER B1: the cap closes the connection silently

`trip` calls `PC.Close()`. `peerStateChanged` treats the closed state with
`removePeer` alone; only the failed state goes through `dropLostPeer` →
`notifyLost`. I checked it with a probe: after the sender cap tripped, the
peer's `OnLost` callback did not fire within 15 s, and the test client's
connection went to `closed`.

So the hub is never told:

- The member keeps the call seat, and the others see them in the call.
- The member gets no `voice_kicked` and no notice. The web client shows
  `announceNoMediaConnection` only on an ICE `failed` state, and nothing on
  `closed`.
- Nothing rejoins. Even `connection_lost` would only end the call with a
  notice; the client does not rejoin by itself.

H-L8-07's caps use the same `trip`, but only a hostile member can reach those,
and only that member is hurt. The sender cap is the first one that an honest
member, or another member (B2), can push a connection into.

What an honest member can reach:

- Leaving the Talk view while in the call (`useVisibleCameras`,
  `onBeforeUnmount`) turns every camera off; coming back turns them all on
  again after 120 ms. In a Talk with 40 cameras that is 40 senders per return.
  32 768 senders is about 820 returns: one every 35 s over 8 hours, or one a
  minute over a 13-hour day.
- On top of that comes passive churn: other members' cameras, joins and
  resumes.

So the cap is unlikely to be hit by honest use, but not out of reach. The
author's estimate of about 15 000 for a working day does not count view
switching.

Fix: send the cap's close through the lost path (`notifyLost`, so the hub
kicks with `connection_lost` and the seat is freed). Better still, let the
client recover by itself: the web client already has a resume rejoin
(`rejoinAfterReconnect`, `resume: true`, which the server treats as no
leave/join within its grace time). An honest member then sees at most a short
media gap.

### BLOCKER B2: other members can drive a viewer's connection to the cap

Senders made because a publisher's track starts or resumes count against the
**viewer's** cap. A publisher can stop its camera with one event and keep
sending; the track is published again after `resumeHoldoff` (500 ms). Measured
on loopback: about 1.9 new senders a second on every viewer's connection,
using well under the event budget. So one member with ordinary rights to send
video closes every other member's media connection after about 4.7 hours,
again and again. Screen shares (for their viewers) and more accounts make it
faster. With B1 the close is silent.

Without the cap, the same flapping now costs each viewer only about 8 MB over
those 4.7 hours. The cap turns a bounded memory cost into a way to cut off
other members' media.

Fix (either is enough with B1's recovery in place; without it, both are
needed):

- Bound the driver at the publisher: a budget for stop/resume cycles per
  source, for example a few per minute, or a holdoff that grows with repeated
  cycles. Then passive churn per viewer stays at honest levels.
- Or count toward the cap only the senders the viewer's own subscriptions
  make, and bound the passive ones by the publisher's budget.

### SHOULD-FIX

- **S1, metric reason.** `rtcp_ssrcs` now also counts sender-cap closes. The
  doc and the metric comment explain this, so it is not wrong. But B1 and B2
  make this cap one that honest members and other members' actions can reach,
  and operators need to tell those closes from hostile RTCP. Give it its own
  reason value (`senders`).
- **S2, CI cost.** The new smoke takes 50.2 s under `-race` on this machine
  (the author measured 49 s): inside the 60 s smoke rule, but with only about
  10 s to spare on a fast M-series Mac. CI runners are usually slower and may
  go past 60 s. The package under `-race` takes 94.0 s on the base `e365c714d`
  and 146.7 s on the tip (+52.6 s, +56%). CI runs it under `-race` twice (the
  unit job and the coverage/integration job), and `internal/sfu` is likely the
  slowest package, so that is about +50 s per job.

  Options:
  - Run the 2 000-switch slope in the nightly deep job and keep a cheaper smoke
    in PR CI. A deterministic check works without a heap slope, for example:
    no stopped sender stays reachable (a weak pointer or a finalizer on a
    stopped `RTPSender`).
  - Or keep the heap slope only in a non-race job.
- **S3, file size.** `internal/sfu/ingress.go` is at 381 of 400 lines; the
  next change there will need a split.

### NOTE

- **Lock order of sentTrack and trip in addSender:** correct. `sentTrack`
  updates the counts under `c.mu` and releases it before `trip`. `trip` takes
  no lock: an atomic load of the connection, a CAS on `closing`, and
  `PC.Close` in its own goroutine. `addSender` runs under the peer's
  `signalingMu`; nothing on the close path takes `signalingMu`, so there is no
  deadlock. `-race` is clean.
- **Hostile cap-and-reconnect loop:** each round costs at most about 8 MB,
  built over 100 to 200 s and freed when the connection closes. In my probe,
  30 rounds left +2 MB and +0 goroutines once the offer watchdog's 10 s window
  had passed; about 1 MB per closed peer stays reachable until then, as
  documented in `cycle_leak_test.go`. Joining an existing Talk is not rate
  limited (only Create-a-Talk lobbies are), so the steady state is about 8 MB
  per hostile member at any time, plus the renegotiation CPU of ordinary
  toggling.
- **Commit message figures:** "320 senders a second" is the event-budget upper
  bound; the measured pace on loopback was lower, so the per-second figures in
  SECURITY.md are conservative. Fine.

## Round 2: tip a15ea9e18 (after f4e479574, the merge of fix-h-l8-07 at d77aa2893)

### B1: a cap close is no longer silent

`peerStateChanged` now hands a closed connection to `closedPeer`, which reports
a cap close as lost. A sender-cap close is marked as a renewal
(`Peer.Renew`):

- the hub closes the member's socket with code 4002,
- the seat is kept for the grace period,
- the client reconnects after 1 s and resumes with `voice_join`,
  `resume: true`.

The abuse caps take the lost path and end the voice with `voice_kicked`
`connection_lost`.

The author's tests for this pass: `TestSenderCapRenewsTheConnection`,
`TestUnhandledSRTCPStreamsAreCapped`, `TestSenderCapRenewsTheMembersConnection`
and `TestAbuseCapEndsTheMembersVoice`.

**What a renewal costs the member, for about one to two seconds.** I read the
socket's unregister path (`lifecycle.go`, `forget`) and the client's send
path:

- **Media:** a gap of about 1 to 2 s. The seat stays, and no
  `voice_state_update` is sent (the author's ws test checks this).
- **Presence:** when the renewed socket is the member's only connection,
  `forget` broadcasts `presence_update` `offline` at once, and `online`
  follows on reconnect. Other members see a flicker. The chosen status
  (dnd, focus) survives: the handshake restores it from the account. This is
  what every dropped socket already does (NOTE R2-1).
- **Events sent during the gap:** `sendSocketEvent` drops them silently
  (typing, speaking, a subscription change). Messages, edits, pins and
  uploads go over HTTP and are not affected. The bot gateway is a separate
  endpoint and is not touched.
- **Per-user gates:** on the last connection, the voice, stage and reaction
  gates and the user's event budgets are reset. A renewal needs at least about
  100 s of sender making, so this is negligible.

**Old web clients and the desktop client:**

- `web/src/stores/socket.ts` is unchanged since v3.0.9. Any close code except
  4001 reconnects after 1 s, and 3.0.9's `App.vue` already runs
  `rejoinAfterReconnect` on `reconnectCount`, with `voice_join`,
  `resume: true`. So a 3.0.9 client handles 4002 correctly without knowing
  it.
- Desktop repository (read only, `d5cde742`): the Linux client's socket proxy
  (`linux/src/core/sockets.ts`) and the experimental one (`web_ws.rs`) pass
  the instance's close code through to the page, which runs the same web
  socket store. So it behaves the same.
- Not tested in a real browser or a real desktop build.

**API stability:**

- `doc/api-stability.md` does not list close codes. A new close code is
  additive under its rule for enum values ("clients must ignore what they do
  not know"), and old clients do in fact treat it as an ordinary drop.
- It is documented in the WebSocket description (`lifecycle.go`), and
  `api/openapi.json` was regenerated: `make openapi-check` exits 0.
- NOTE R2-2: the bot gateway already uses 4002 with another meaning (a bad
  frame, `doc/bots.md`). That is legal on a separate endpoint, but one line in
  `doc/bots.md` or the WebSocket description saying the codes are per

**Test seam.** `Peer.TripCap` is defined only in `internal/sfu/testhooks.go`.
A search of the whole tree finds no caller outside `_test.go` files. It is an

### B2: the resume budget bounds what others can drive

Each track now has a resume budget: 10 quick resumes, then one per 10 s.

- **Worst case asked for (50 publishers, camera and screen each, within the

- SHOULD-FIX R2-3: the budget lives on the `TrackInfo`. A capture that is
  replaced (a new track for the same source) or a publisher that rejoins

  by publisher and source in the room would make it survive replacement and

**Honest use:**

- Camera visibility (`cameraVisibility.ts`, `useVisibleCameras`) only changes
  the viewer's subscriptions (`webrtc_subscribe`); it never stops the
  publisher's track.
- Mute and push-to-talk set `track.enabled` (`audioSettings.ts`) and send no
  stop event. Audio is never unpublished by a client stop.
- Only a camera switched off by hand (`camera.ts`, `webrtc_camera_stop`) and

**Past the budget:** viewers see the publisher's camera tile without a picture
for up to 10 s (the media state still says the camera is on). The publisher
sees its own preview and gets no notice. Acceptable for something that needs
more than 10 camera toggles in quick succession (NOTE R2-4: a hint to the

### The resume budget per publisher and source

`Room.resumeBudgets` is keyed by publisher and source and is guarded by
`r.mu`. It is reached only through `unpublish` → `stopSourceTracksLocked` →
`stopUntilLocked`, under the room's write lock, and it reads only `r.peers`
and the map. No other lock is taken, so the lock order is r.mu only.

- **One stop event spends one resume**, however many tracks it ends (layers,
  the share's sound): `resumeAt` is computed once per stop.
- **The clamp at −1 does not allow faster cycling.** A stop acts only on live
  tracks, and over budget a track resumes only once the balance has refilled
  from −1 to 0, 10 s later. So the steady rate through resumes stays at one
  per 10 s per source, as before. Without the clamp, repeated stops could only
  push the balance lower than −1 if new captures of the same source kept
  arriving while one was held. The clamp caps that wait at 10 s; it gives no
  extra resumes.
- **Rejoins and replaced captures no longer reset the budget.** An entry of a
  publisher who left is deleted only once it has refilled to full, so
  forgetting it never grants more than the refill would have.
  `TestResumeBudgetOutlivesCapturesAndRejoins` passes. Revert check: with a
  fresh budget per stop (the key built from a new UUID) it fails, with 200
  quick resumes against at most 17.
- **Measured again on the live path:** 5 publishers stopping and resuming
  their cameras as fast as allowed for 60 s made 77 senders on one viewer,
  15.4 per source: the burst of 10 plus about 6 refills, 0.1 a second per
  source steady. The map held 5 entries. The worst case asked for (50
  publishers, camera and screen) is unchanged from round 2: about 10 senders a
  second per viewer, the cap after about 53 minutes, then a renewal.

### Bound of the map when nobody stops again

Entries are only created by a stop: screen and camera by the publisher's own
stop, and the microphone through a permission revoke (`RevokeUserSource`).
They are only deleted inside a later stop in the same room. So if nobody ever
stops again, entries stay until the room is closed, which happens when it is
empty.

- **Bound:** at most 3 entries per distinct member who stopped a source while
  the room stayed open (one per source), about 120 to 150 bytes each (a
  16-byte UUID and a string header as the key, a pointer, and a
  `resumeBudget` of 32 bytes, plus map overhead).
- **Example:** a room that never empties for months, with 10 000 distinct
  members each stopping two sources, holds about 20 000 entries, roughly 3 MB.
  One account can never hold more than 3 entries.
- **Cost of the sweep:** the sweep inside `stopUntilLocked` is linear in the
  map, but stops themselves are budgeted (about 0.1 a second per source), so
  it stays negligible.

NOTE R3-1: acceptable as is. If rooms that never close matter, a sweep on peer
removal would bound the map by the members seated in the last 110 s, not by
those ever seated.
