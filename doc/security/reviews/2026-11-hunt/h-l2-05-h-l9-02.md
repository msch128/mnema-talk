## Part A: fix review of `f8e6e5667`

### Verdict: MERGE

### What I checked

- The change: `pacer.ts` and `backoff.ts` default to `clock.now()` through a closure (so a test that replaces `clock.now` is followed);
  the four construction sites pass it explicitly (`browserCall.ts:15`, `groupBase.ts:56,60,61`). No `new WirePacer(` or
  `new Backoff(` is left without it (grep over non-test code). The diff of the commit outside tests and comments is exactly those
  files: nothing else of the protocol changed.
- Remaining wall readers in non-test `lib/media`: `clock.ts:14` (fallback when there is no `performance`), `clock.ts:20`
  (`clock.wall`), `groupEpoch.ts:106,184` (`lastTickWall`) and `groupBase.ts:318` (`sleepGap`, the larger of the monotonic and the wall
  gap). That is the one deliberate wall reader the author names; a step back cannot move it (the wall gap goes negative, the
  monotonic gap wins), a step forward can only make a tab look as if it slept (see seed 120 below).
- Revert checks (four production files restored to the parent, then each alone):

  | Reverted | Result |
  | --- | --- |
  | all four files | 3 tests fail (pacer sends 5 of 10, back-off waits 3603000 ms, the room is apart for 314 and 1304 s) |
  | `pacer.ts` only | `pacer.step.test.ts` fails |
  | `backoff.ts` only | `backoff.test.ts` fails |
  | `groupBase.ts` only (the three construction sites) | nothing fails |
  | `browserCall.ts` only (the pacer's construction site) | nothing fails |

  The last two are not a defect: the defaults are monotonic now, so passing the clock explicitly is redundant, and the tests
  fail when a default turns back to the wall clock. It is the one thing the tests do not pin: a site that later builds its own clock.
- Gates: `src/lib/media` 48 files passed, 6 skipped, 450 tests passed, 87 skipped (exit 0); ESLint on `src/lib/media` exit 0;
  `vue-tsc --noEmit` for `tsconfig.app.json` and `tsconfig.tests.json` exit 0. `groupBase.ts` 344 lines, `pacer.ts` 61,
  `backoff.ts` 41.

### The claim about `performance.now` across sleep

The commit says both behaviours are safe and I agree with the argument in the code. `monotonicNow` is `performance.timeOrigin +
performance.now()`. Whether `performance.now()` advances while the machine sleeps depends on the browser and the OS (it
does on some platforms and does not on others, which is why the sleep check reads the wall clock too). Standing still: a wait
resumes after waking with its awake remainder, never longer than its own length (pacer one second; back-offs at most their
ceiling, 60 s for swaps and resets, 10 minutes for rivals). Jumping forward: the wait is over, as the time really passed, and
a back-off that is quiet starts from its base. A backward move cannot happen. I did not run a real browser; this is the code
and the specifications.

### Findings

- NOTE 1. After a sleep during which the monotonic clock stood still, a rival back-off opened just before the sleep still holds
  for its remaining awake time, up to 10 minutes (`RIVAL_MAX_MS`), where the wall clock would have released it. That delays the
  merge of two groups after a wake by up to the ceiling; it cannot loop and is inside the constants. Acceptable.
- NOTE 2. The tests do not pin the construction sites (table above). A one-line test that builds a `MediaGroup` and checks that
  its three back-offs follow a replaced `clock.now` would. Not needed to merge.
- NOTE 3. SECURITY.md already says the key schedule is measured with the monotonic clock (line 948) and does not mention the
  pacer or the back-offs, so no text is wrong. The remaining wall reader (sleep check) is described in the commit message and the
  `clock.ts` header only; one sentence in SECURITY.md would complete it.
- NOTE 4 (not about this commit). SECURITY.md in the merged tree says "A receiver never accepted a removal earlier than the limit".
  The rule is `now - lastAlive <= absentMs - slack` (`groupVet.ts`, `prematureRemoval`): a removal is accepted from `limit minus
  one maintenance interval` of silence by the receiver's own clock (19.5 minutes in production, 28 s in the simulator). The
  sentence should say "never earlier than the limit minus one maintenance interval". Seed 20 below is a consequence.

### What I did not check

A real browser; the heartbeat behaviour of the desktop client; whether any code outside `lib/media` builds a pacer or a back-off
(grep of `web/src`: none).

## Part B: the availability budget

### Method

The nightly availability sweep (`SIM_AVAIL=1 SIM_SHARD=<i>/8 SIM_AVAIL_OUT=...`, `groupAvailability.sweep.test.ts`), all 8 shards,
both relays (50 seeds each per relay per shard), at the merged tree and with H-L2-04's retry switched off (the first line of

### Judgement

Acceptable as is, with the table set to the merged tree's numbers.

1. The effect is not systematic. Over 400 hostile seeds the paired difference of the lost-key share has mean +0.88 points and a
   standard deviation of 9.4 points per seed (a standard error of about 0.5), while the member-time-weighted pool is 12.74 to 12.82
   percent. 230 of 400 hostile seeds change trajectory (17 of 400 on the honest relay) and they move up and down in equal measure:
   a shard of 50 seeds swings by about 1.3 points just from the reshuffle, so the budget's window of 2 points per shard is around
   1.5 standard deviations of what any change to the group's timing does. Shard 0 (+2.8 points) and shard 6 (-1.9) are the two ends
   of that spread.
2. The intended price. The retry rule makes a member that refused a commit for an unseen add start over after 4 s; the member
   without keys is then counted in the metric, where a member left behind with stale keys was not. Over the 400 hostile seeds
   the new path fired 2120 holds and 261 start-overs (of 5682 start-overs in all, 4.6 percent); 88 percent of the holds were
   resolved by the package arriving. On the honest relay 157 holds and 28 start-overs (1.3 percent). Both relays: no
   invariant broke; the room agrees at least as fast at the median.
3. No liveness defect found in the fix. In seed 352 the new path made one start-over (m2 at 15.5 s) and 19 holds, the last at 76 s, none of which led to a start-over after 15.5 s; the long stretch
   (four members without keys from 75 s to 217 s) is not a start-over of the new path.
   The trajectory differs from the first start-over on, and the members' stretch comes from the chain of
   leaf swaps by stale packages (`commit[remove,add]` every epoch) that the hostile relay's replays cause, which lane2-b has as
   well on other seeds (its worst is 34 percent). Same count of start-overs in both runs (44 and 43), different timing. The retry and
   the start-over fire only through the flag "the first violation was an unseen add" (reviewed earlier); a hold that is not
   an unseen add cannot start anything (a mutant test kills that).
   A hostile relay cannot keep a member out for good by this path where it could not before: the start-over is bounded by
   the reset limit, the relay can already stop any member's package from arriving, and a member that cannot start over because
   of the limit stays behind exactly as before.
4. Set the table to the merged numbers. Hostile `shardLost`: `[0.1505, 0.1067, 0.1295, 0.1373, 0.1114, 0.1179, 0.148, 0.1108]`;
   honest: `[0.0643, 0.046, 0.0581, 0.0517, 0.0593, 0.0669, 0.0738, 0.0646]`. The per-seed limit for a seed outside the failure budget
   (`seed: 0.35` for hostile) is below three measured seeds (38.9, 38.2, 36.3): raise it to 0.40 or the nightly will fail on seeds
   187, 352 and 107. The table is new, so this is setting a budget, not lowering a gate; say in the comment that it was measured
   on the merged tree and that per-shard values swing by about 1.3 points with any change of timing.
   Optional improvement: make the nightly check the pooled value over all 8 shards (12.8 hostile, 6.1 honest, window 1 point)
   rather than each shard alone, which is the statistic that did not move.

I did not run lane2-b itself (a second checkout), only its behaviour in the merged code, which reproduces its committed table to
four digits.

## Part C: the five seeds

All traced with a scratch runner on the merged tree (`runSeed` with the plan named). I give the repro of the one real defect in
`findings/H-L2-06.md` (in the repository: [doc/security/findings/2026-11-hunt/h-l2-06.md](../../findings/2026-11-hunt/h-l2-06.md)).

### 1. Seed 5, hostile, every plan with steps: a real defect, one finding (H-L2-06)

End state at 315 s: two groups with the same members `{1,7}`, both at epoch 14, different keys
(`m7#4:14:... {1,7} | m1#8:14:... {1,7}`). It fails with plan `steps`, `steps-back` and `all`, and passes with `none`, as the author says; with
the wall steps made no-ops it fails too (I made `stepWall` a no-op and ran it: still fails), so it is not the clock.

It is not permanent: with the room given 2 hours of virtual time it agrees 486 s after the relay stops (at the simulator's compressed
lifetimes). At production ratios the same seed and plan settle (a different history).

Why the two groups never see each other, from the trace and the code: both groups have the same ids and the same epoch NUMBER. A
heartbeat names the group, the id, the epoch number and a time (`heartbeat.ts`), not the tree's state; the leaf of the lowest id in
each group is signed with the same key, so its heartbeats verify in the other group and count as a sign of life when the numbers are equal
(SECURITY.md says so: "when the two epoch numbers happen to be equal; they make nobody start over"). The detector for
another state needs the numbers to differ at two heartbeats at least 8 s apart with the member's own state unchanged in between, while
both groups rotate and renew. Welcomes do not help: m7's welcome to m1#8 is ignored because m1#8's committer is itself, and m1#8's welcome
to m7 is not from "a lower id outside my group" (its sender id is the id of m7's committer, rivalry being judged by id, not leaf).
A zombie leaf of the lowest id in the other group therefore stays alive for as long as the numbers coincide.

Belongs on the known-failure list with the finding id H-L2-06 (the list comment should say "equal-epoch fork", not "the plan's
schedule"). Not a harness matter.

### 2. Seed 120 of plan "all", HONEST relay: the same defect; not permanent; the sleep check moves the history, it is not the cause

End state: `{2,4,6}` (m4, m6) and `{2,4,6,11}` (m2, m11), both at epoch 14, different keys: the same equal-epoch fork as seed 5, now on an honest relay.
It is not permanent: with 2 hours it agrees after 304 s (the invariant allows 240 s).

Is the sleep check the cause? I ran three variants. With the wall step made a no-op (`stepWall` returns before it touches the clock) the seed passes (agrees after 9 s). With the step applied but the
simulator's "woke at" mark removed, it still fails. With `sleepGap` ignoring the wall clock, it passes (that also breaks sleep
detection for the sleepers, so it only says the history changes). So: the forward step of 447 s on m9 at 16.7 s is read by m9's sleep
check as a sleep (its wall gap exceeds the sleep limit): m9 resets its signs of life, skips a commit, and takes a removal on trust for a minute; m9 leaves 20 s later, and the
different history (another committer order, another swap at 59.6 s) ends in the fork. The sleep check is deliberate and documented
(a forward step at most marks the tab as slept); the fork it leads to is H-L2-06. I did not trace the fork's genesis step by step.

Belongs in the failure list under H-L2-06; the comment "which the sleep check reads as a sleep" is true but suggests a different
cause than the one that blocks the heal.

### 3. drift/hostile 20 and extreme/honest 82: harness matters, not defects

- Seed 20, plan `drift` (0.5 percent rate error), hostile: "took the removal of m4, who is in the room and was heard 27901 ms ago".
  The limit is 30 s and the receiver's rule refuses only while the silence is at most `limit - slack`, with a slack of one
  maintenance interval (2 s in the simulator): it accepts from 28 s by its own clock. With its clock 0.5 percent fast, 28 s on its clock is
  27.86 s of real time; 27.9 s is exactly there. The checker's tolerance is 2 s and does not add the drift. So the removal is
  inside the protocol's rule plus the clock error: the oracle's tolerance should be `slack + drift * limit`, or the plan's
  removal tolerance widened; or (honestly) the sentence in SECURITY.md should be "the limit minus one interval" (Part A note 4).
  It passed before only because the history differed.
- Seed 82, plan `extreme` (3 percent), honest: "took a commit from m5 although m3 commits now". The plan's header says it is far beyond real hardware
  and "here to show that the oracle can see a clock fault at all"; with 3 percent a member's patience for a silent lowest id
  (`limit + 2 intervals`) ends a second early in real time (34 s against 33 s). It will fail on some seeds whenever the history
  changes. Not a defect. Either add the seed to the known list with a comment "extreme plan, 3 percent, harness" or give the plan's checker the same
  drift allowance as above. I did not trace the seed further.

Neither belongs under a finding id; both are harness matters (the oracle's tolerance is not clock-aware) with the SECURITY.md wording note above.

### 4. liar/hostile 102: a slow tail that H-L2-04's timing moved over the line; cause not traced; no new finding yet

On the merged tree it agrees after 284 s (the limit is 240 s): at 315 s `m4#3` still holds an old epoch (30) while the room is at epoch 86 (its
violation text calls it "no group"; it holds keys of its old group). It heals at 359 s. Variants (all with the roster-lying plan on the hostile relay):
merged tree 284 s (fails); without H-L2-04's retry, with the clock fix, 164 s (passes); with H-L2-04 and the clock fix
reverted, fails; neither fix, 164 s (passes). So it is H-L2-04, not the clock fix, that moves it: with the retry switched off, the seed passes.
The new path did not start anybody over in this run (12 holds, each resolved by the package arriving; no start-over at all), so what moved is the timing of
12 commits delayed by 4 s, and the run ends in a different slow tail: a member behind the room for four minutes, receiving commits from the
lowest id (m1#7, a reloaded tab) that it does not take, and not starting over. I suspect (not shown) that "a commit ahead" is only accepted when the
sender's leaf index matches the leaf the member's old tree holds for that id, which fails after the lowest id reloaded, leaving the
member to wait for the heartbeat detector or the zombie leaf's removal. This should become a finding after a reproducer (the question to
answer is why m4 does not start over on the commits ahead from m1#7 between 75 s and 359 s).

Meanwhile it belongs on the failure list (liar/hostile: `[102]`) as "slow tail (284 s), moved by H-L2-04's timing; cause open", which is
honest, since it passes without H-L2-04 and heals on its own. It is also a harness matter in part: the 240 s of the invariant is
a constant of the compressed timing.
