## A. H-L8-02: per-member allocation budget on the built-in TURN relay

Verdict: **MERGE.**

### Does it remove the cause?

Yes. `allowAllocation` (`internal/turnrelay/handlers.go:125-141`) now keys `allocLimit` by the member id parsed from the credential username. Before, it was keyed by the source address. The cause the refuter named was a budget keyed by something the server cannot attribute, and that cause is gone. A member who forges any number of sources draws on one bucket only. The 4096-key table can then fill only with real, distinct members.

I read pion/turn v5.1.3 `handleAllocateRequest` and `authenticateRequest` (`internal/server/turn.go`, `util.go`) to check the order the author relies on:
1. A request without MESSAGE-INTEGRITY gets a 401 with a nonce. No handler of ours runs.
2. A bad or stale nonce gets a 401 or 438. No handler of ours runs.
3. The AuthHandler (`authenticate`) runs. It uses `failLimit.available`, then `failLimit.allow` only for a malformed or expired username. This is unchanged.
4. The integrity check runs. A forged signature ends here, and `OnAuth` fires with verdict false. Our handler returns at once on false.
5. On success, `OnAuth(true)` fires, then the 5-tuple retry check (step 2 of RFC 5766), then the attribute checks, then the QuotaHandler (step 7), and only then CreateAllocation.

So dropping the per-source allocation table opens no unauthenticated path. Nothing before the QuotaHandler ever touched `allocLimit`, and `failLimit` keeps its per-source-and-username shape. The finding's TCP question is settled the same way. TURN over TCP uses the same server-level QuotaHandler, so it gets the same per-member key, and `limitedListener` keeps its per-address connection caps.

Variants:
- Forged sources from one member: fixed (`TestOneMemberFromThousandsOfSourcesCannotKeepAnotherOut`).
- A banned member saturating with a still-valid credential: now refused in the quota handler before the budget is charged (`TestABannedMembersCredentialIsRefusedBeforeTheLimiter`).
- TCP clients sharing the table: fixed, because the table is now per member.

### Own probes (not in the tests)

1. **Two members behind one address** each got their full burst of 60 from the same source. The office NAT case is not penalised.
2. **One member's flood of 10,000 signed Allocates from 10,000 sources** caused exactly **1** account lookup. The 8 s verdict cache and the single flight hold.
3. **A check that returns an error** (database down) fails closed. The request is refused, the bucket is not charged, and the error is not cached: 100 requests gave 100 lookups. See NOTE A-2.
4. **A malformed username** reaching the quota handler is refused. This path is unreachable after `authenticate` anyway.
5. **Eviction profit with many real accounts** (the lead's probe). K accounts each drain their bucket every second for 30 s against `newMemberLimiter(120, 60, 4096)`. I compared the attempts granted with the budget K accounts have without eviction, K × (60 + 2 × 29):

   | K accounts | granted | budget without eviction | ratio |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | 4096 | 483,328 | 483,328 | 1.00 |
   | 4097 | 501,368 | 483,446 | 1.04 |
   | 5000 | 5,191,430 | 590,000 | 8.8 |
   | 8192 | 13,429,696 | 966,656 | 13.9 |

   See NOTE A-1.

### Findings

- **NOTE A-1: a cohort of more than 4096 real accounts resets its own budgets.**
  - `limit.go:69-88` makeRoom evicts the bucket with the most tokens. When every bucket is drawn down, an evicted member comes back with a full burst of 60.
  - From 4097 accounts on, a group cycling through the table gets more attempts than its accounts' budgets add up to: 8.8 times with 5000 accounts, 13.9 times with 8192 (probe 5).
  - Not a blocker:
    - Registration is invite-only, and each account needs voice rights. Thousands of accounts on one community is an insider with admin-visible scale.
    - What the attacker gains is allocation *attempts*. Real allocations stay capped at 4 per member and 256 in total.
    - No other member is ever refused because of the eviction, which was the property the finding was about.
    - Each eviction costs a 4096-entry scan under the limiter mutex, which runs inline in pion's read loop. That is microseconds.
  - The documentation ("the fullest one is forgotten rather than a new member refused") is accurate. The commit message's "the forgotten bucket lost the least" is true per eviction but not over a cycle.
  - Possible hardening: give an evicted key back only what it had, for example a small "recently evicted" tombstone. Optional.
- **NOTE A-2: a failed account check now runs ahead of the rate limit.**
  - `handlers.go:130` asks `mayJoin` before `allocLimit`. Errors are not cached (`handlers.go:83-90`), and the check has a 2 s timeout (`internal/server/router_build.go:296`).
  - While the database is down or slow, every signed Allocate from a member waits for a fresh lookup of up to 2 s inside pion's single read loop for that listener. Before the fix, this happened only for requests within the per-source budget.
  - This is not a regression in practice:
    - Before the fix, a member within budget (60 burst, then 2/s) could already keep the loop waiting on lookups of up to 2 s each.
    - A spoofing member was not limited at all.
    - The verdict is cached for 8 s whenever the database answers.
  - The commit message's "Cost on the packet path" does not mention the error case.
- **NOTE A-3: OnAuth starts one goroutine per verified request.** This is pre-existing code, unchanged by this fix (`handlers.go:107-114`, `events().OnAuth`).
  - Probe: one lookup hanging for 2 s, then 20,000 calls of the `OnAuth` handler with verdict true for one member.
  - Result: **+20,000 goroutines**, back to +0 after the lookup returned.
  - So the count is bounded by the signed-request rate × the lookup duration (at most the 2 s timeout). It grows only while a lookup is in flight, which is normally milliseconds every 8 s, or always while the database fails.
  - Every request of an allocation that passes the integrity check fires `OnAuth`: Refresh, CreatePermission and ChannelBind as well as Allocate. A member flooding signed CreatePermission or Refresh requests during a database outage therefore adds about rate × 2 s goroutines. Send indications carry no integrity and do not count.
  - It is bounded, so it is not a growth path without limit, and it needs a valid credential.
  - Cheap hardening for a follow-up: under `s.mu`, check whether a verdict is fresh or a flight exists, and spawn only when neither holds. Then at most one goroutine per member at a time.
  - I recommend a follow-up rather than blocking this fix.
- **NOTE A-4: established allocations of a banned member.**
  - They are dropped at the first definite "no" from `mayJoin` (`DropMember`, `handlers.go:96-101`). That happens on the member's next signed request after the 8 s verdict expires: a permission refresh, a channel bind refresh or an allocation refresh, which browsers send every few minutes.
  - Nothing calls `DropMember` at the moment of the ban (grep: the only caller is `handlers.go`).
  - This is unchanged by the fix, and the documentation does not claim more.
- **NOTE A-5: benchmark write-up.** `doc/benchmark.md:388-392` describes the relay limit ("accepts 120 allocations per minute") as shaping the load-test setup. That was the per-address limit, and it is now per member. It is a historical measurement, so the change is optional: say "per member" or "at the time, per address".

## B. H-C-01 and H-C-02: mention kinds by the strongest allowed reason

Verdict: **MERGE AFTER FIXES.**

The server half is correct for every human member, and nothing that was right before changed. But H-C-02's documented effect, a muted member reached through a plain-text name, still happens in the web client: live badge, desktop notification and sound. After a reload, the client now disagrees with the server. A bot named next to @all is still notified.

### What the fix does

- `addRoleMentions` (`mentions_roles.go:64-99`) raises a member who is already in the set at kind group to kind role. A name (kind 0) stays.
- `allowedKind` (`mentions.go:107-115`) demotes kind 0 to group under `NoUsers`, unless the member is in `AllowUsers`.
- The order inside resolveMentionSet: names and @all first, then allowedMentions, which demotes, then addRoleMentions, which raises. So a demoted member who holds a mentioned role ends at kind role. That is right.

### Every reader of the kind, checked against the author's table

- **readstate** (`readstate.go:54-56`): `mention_count` counts `kind <> group`, or any kind unless `suppress_everyone`. With the fix:
  - A muted role member counts "@all @Dev". This is the H-C-01 fix.
  - A member who suppresses @all and @here now also counts the role. This matches the client's rule for suppress_everyone (`messageMentionsMeDirectly` reads `mention_roles`), so a former mismatch is fixed as a side effect.
- **push** `namedMentions` (`recipients.go:174-197`): `kind <> group`. `TestPushFollowsTheStoredMentionKinds` covers both directions.
- **thread follow** (`thread_follow.go:41-55`, `thread_follow_sync.go:79`): `kind <> group`.
  - A role member reached by "@all @Dev" in a reply now follows the thread, as for "@Dev" alone. This is consistent.
  - A member named next to @all by a users-off webhook no longer follows. This is consistent with the policy.
- **inbox** (`search/inbox.go`) and the `mentions` list (`messages.go:136-149`) ignore the kind, so nothing changes there.
- **cap** (`mentions_cap.go`):
  - `capRecipients` cuts only under `NoEveryone`. Then there is no @all or @here, so no group kind exists to raise or to create. Demotion needs a member that mentionSkipped kept, which under NoEveryone means an AllowUsers member, and those keep kind 0. So the 50-recipient cut and its kind ordering are unchanged.
  - `capNames` (100 names) runs before all of this.
- **web client** (`web/src/stores/chat/notifications.ts:37-46`, `events.ts:144-146`): a muted channel counts a message when the server's `mentions` list contains me **and** (the text names me **or** a role of mine is in `mention_roles`). See BLOCKER B-1.

Combinations that were right before and are still right (own integration probe, plus the author's table, which passes):
- An ordinary "@all" in an unmuted channel: everyone at kind group, badge 1.
- A name mention by a member: kind 0.
- "@here @Dev" with an offline role member: kind role. The member is reached by the role alone.
- "@here @all @Dev @NAMEDMUTED" from the owner (my own text, upper-case name):
  - plain members: group
  - role holders: role
  - the named member: name
  - the other named members (not in this text): group
  - the offline role member: role
  - the offline named member: group
- Edit "@all @Dev" to "@all only": the role members go back to group, so edits recompute.

### Findings

- **BLOCKER B-1 (H-C-02 is not fixed for the web client).**
  - Scenario: a webhook with `allow_user_mentions` off and `allow_everyone` on posts "@all @max" to a channel max has muted.
    - The server now stores max at kind group, so the badge after a reload is 0 and there is no push. That is correct.
    - The WebSocket `message_create` still lists max in `mentions`, because @all reaches max. The text names max, so `messageMentionsMeDirectly` returns true.
    - So the **live badge shows 1, a desktop notification is raised, and the mention sound plays** in the muted channel. After a reload the badge is 0.
  - Probe in the web store (a copy of `chatMentionPolicy.test.ts`'s setup, payload `mentions: ['me']`, content "@all @max deploy", channel muted). Expected count 0 and no notification. Got:

    ```
    expected { count: 1, notes: 1 } to deeply equal { count: +0, notes: +0 }
    ```

  - Before the fix, client and server agreed: both counted 1, and both were wrong. After it they disagree. That is the "live badge and badge after reload disagree" symptom H-C-01 described, now for H-C-02.
  - The new upgrade note and the webhooks.md sentence ("a named member is reached by that group only, which a mute silences") are not true for the web client's live state and desktop notification.
  - The same holds for the bot route (a bot whose `allowed_mentions` leaves out `users` and does not list the member).
  - The client cannot tell from the payload whether a name was allowed. A fix needs the server to say so, for example a list of the members addressed by name or role, or the kind per entry of `mentions`. Then `messageMentionsMeDirectly` reads that list instead of matching the text. The 3.0.9 comment on that function already says it wants exactly this ("a name it left as plain text addresses nobody").
  - The fix also needs a web test mirroring `chatMentionPolicy.test.ts` for "@all @max" from a users-off sender.
  - This is a client and payload change, so the desktop-client coupling rule may apply if the desktop client reads `mentions`.
- **SHOULD-FIX B-2: a bot named next to @all under NoUsers is still notified, now at a kind that cannot exist.** This is the author's own suspicion, and it is confirmed.
  - Probe: a users-off webhook (allow_everyone on, parse `["everyone","roles"]`).
    - It posts "@all @<bot username> ping". The bot is **stored at kind 1 (group)** and is in the message's `mentions` list.
    - The same webhook posts "@<bot username> ping" alone. The bot is **not mentioned**.
  - Cause: `mentionSkipped` (`mentions.go:120-128`) keeps every id when `targets.all` is set. For a bot, though, the SQL result (`mentions.go:164-170`) is there only because of the name. "@all and @here never include bots" (`doc/bots.md:88`).
  - Before the fix the bot got kind 0. Now it gets kind 1. Either way the bot sees its id in `MESSAGE_CREATE.mentions`, which is how doc/bots.md:84-87 says a bot recognises a mention of itself. It may act on a mention the policy turned into plain text.
  - Harmless for badge, push and thread follow: bots do not read state or subscribe, and kind group does not follow.
  - But it contradicts the new architecture.md sentence ("A name only counts when the sender may name members") and webhooks.md ("off turns every member mention into plain text").
  - Fix: under `NoUsers`, a non-allowed id that the group cannot reach must be skipped, not demoted. For example, let `queryMentioned` return whether the row was reached by the group (`$1 AND NOT is_bot`, or `$3 AND id = ANY($4)`), and demote only those. Drop the rest.
  - Add the bot to the kinds table: "webhook users off @all" gives the bot "-".
- **NOTE B-3: messages stored before the update keep their old kinds** (upgrade note). I find this acceptable.
  - The effect is limited to badges of unread messages and thread follows created from then on.
  - Push has already been sent for old messages.
  - A backfill would need to re-resolve old texts under policies that are no longer known: the webhook's settings at send time and the online set for @here. That cannot be done faithfully.
- **NOTE B-4: "@here @Role".** The reproducer reported that "@here @Ops" did not reproduce (count 1 before the fix). The code before the fix gives an *online* role member kind group through @here and then skips them in addRoleMentions. So "@here @Role" was affected for online members, as the commit message says ("or @here, when online").
  - My inference, not re-run on the parent: the reproducer's probe member had no WebSocket connection. @here did not reach them, so the role alone added them at kind role.
  - After the fix, the kinds table pins kind role for "@here @Dev" both online (`role` column) and offline (`awayrole` column).
