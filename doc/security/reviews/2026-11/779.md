B1. BLOCKER (availability; no key is exposed) -- web/src/lib/media/groupMerge.ts:24-27 (lead), :45-58 (mergeOffer),
    with group.ts:117-128 (receiveReturningMember) and groupSync.ts:40-56 (reachable)
    1b offers to "the room's lowest reachable id" whenever my tree lacks it. Nothing requires that this id holds a group,
    answers, or ever spoke MLS correctly. "Reachable" is: listed by the roster and either in my tree, or EVER sent one
    handshake that parses (heard, never cleared), or first listed less than 8 s ago. And the offer is a key package from
    a member INSIDE a group, which that group's own committer reads as a reload: it swaps the leaf (remove+add) in a
    commit the offering member cannot follow (it refuses the welcome: not a rival, group larger than one). The member
    is out of step with its own group until the next commit makes it start over; then it is welcomed back with a new
    package, which it offers again on the next tick.
    Scenario 1, no malice: the lowest id's tab hangs but stays listed (the case the absence limit and the hung-tab skip
    exist for). Room of 3 formed normally, production timing (30 s tick, 20 min absence), engine start-over modelled:
      #775: present members without a common key for 0 s of the following hour; 4 commits, 0 key packages.
      #779: 1542 s of 3600 s; 83 commits, 171 key packages, 82 welcomes.
      (First reviewer's larger runs on the same head: room of 12: 2314 s of 3600 s, 446 commits, 873 key packages.)
    Scenario 2, one listed member: members 2..4 hold a group; id 1 is listed, sends ONE key package that is not even its
    own (every receiver drops it) and stays silent. Production timing:
      #775: 0 s of the following 30 min without a common key; 3 commits.
      #779: 1599 s of 1800 s; 87 commits, 194 key packages. (Room of 8, first reviewer: 1781 s of 1800 s, five of seven
      members end with no group.)
    Trace (scenario 2, t in s): 90 m2 offers; 93 m3 offers -> m2 (its committer) commits remove+add for m3; 96 m4 offers
    -> swap; m3 sees a commit ahead, starts over, is welcomed; m4 the same; ... repeats every tick for good.
    So ANY listed id below the group (a hung tab, an old or broken client, a member who wants to) keeps the whole Talk
    regrouping, without being in the group and without signing anything. At main/#775 such an id can do nothing: it must
    be let in first, and only then can it misuse the committer role. This also makes SECURITY.md:707-712 false ("a
    handshake that merely parses, a key package ... from an id outside the group never does [discard a group]").
    Even with an honest newcomer there is a small window: a lower id is listed, and a member's tick falls before that
    id's add commit: the member offers, is swapped out by its own committer and starts over at the add (one restart).
    Suggested fix (all three, they are independent):
     (a) The committer must not treat a merge offer as a reload. Cheapest: a member in a group does not broadcast a
         package its own group would swap. E.g. the receiver ignores a non-identical package from a member of its tree
         while the room lists a lower id outside the tree (it is a merge offer by construction), or the offer is marked.
     (b) Offer only to an id that has shown it holds a group and commits in it, not to a merely listed one: e.g. only
         after a welcome-capable sign from it (its own key package announcement is not one; a commit or welcome seen
         from it under that id, or its group-of-one announcement, is closer). At the very least not within TAKEOVER_MS
         of its first listing and not on `heard` alone.
     (c) Give up: after k unanswered offers stop asking that id (doubling wait like `rivals`), so a dead lead costs a
         bounded number of handshakes instead of one swap per member per tick forever.
    Add both scenarios as tests (first reviewer's zzhung.test.ts / zzsilent.test.ts are ready to adapt).

B2. SHOULD-FIX -- web/src/lib/media/group.ts:137 (rivals.allow before the welcome is opened), groupMerge.ts:49
    The once-per-minute allowance for a rival welcome is spent on ANY welcome from the lead, also one built on someone
    else's package (the welcome goes to the whole room). In a split room the lead answers one offer, and that welcome
    locks every other member of the higher group out for 60 s (they do not even ask: mergeOffer checks waitMs); the next
    one doubles the wait for the rest (120, 240, 480, 600 s).
    Measured (first reviewer's zzmerge.test.ts, production timing, honest relay; trace re-read by me, not re-run):
    2+2 members: one group after 87 s; 2+4: 447 s (members merge at 36, 99, 222, 447 s); 6+6: 1527 s (25 min).
    The PR text says "1b within one maintenance interval (30 s) plus the welcome"; that holds for the first member only.
    Fix: check `rivals.waitMs(from) > 0` before, and call `rivals.allow(from)` only after joinGroup succeeded and the
    committer rule passed (the welcome was built on MY package and I am about to discard my group). The limit exists to
    bound how often I drop a group, and a welcome that does not open drops nothing.

B3. SHOULD-FIX -- web/src/lib/media/group.ts:365-371 (beatNow), groupBase.ts:245 (discard clears beats),
    groupMerge.ts:77-88; SECURITY.md:724-736
    1c's replay guard is `beats`, which (i) records only heartbeats for MY epoch, never the two that made me start
    over, and (ii) is emptied by discard() and is empty for a newcomer. So any two genuine heartbeats of the lowest id
    signed with its current leaf key (the signature key does not change while its tab lives), for epochs other than
    mine, can be sent again by the server:
     - to a member that never took them (they were held back): first reviewer's R1, re-run: Carol starts over, is let in
       again, and the same two wires make her start over again, three rounds out of three;
     - to a NEWCOMER, with nothing ever held back (my N1): Alice sent h1 (t=6 s) and h2 (t=20 s) to Bob in the normal
       way; Carol joins later; h1, then h2 8 s later -> Carol starts over ("none", offers a package), is let in again,
       and the same two wires work again in every round (3 of 3). In a long call the server holds dozens of them.
    Bound: the reset backoff only (10 s doubling to 60 s; back to 10 s whenever frames open). Server-only: a member
    cannot send under the lowest id's name. The server can force a start-over at main as well (a commit for a later
    epoch under the committer's id is taken unverified, same backoff), so this is no new capability and I rate it
    should-fix, not blocker. But SECURITY.md's "a copy, an older one ... count for nothing; by holding back the commit
    between two genuine ones it can make me start over" understates it: it needs to hold back nothing.
    The PR's test "keeps its group when heartbeats it took in an earlier epoch are sent again" covers only the case
    where `beats` still holds them.
    Fix: keep the newest heartbeat time per signer key in a map that survives discard() (pruned when the id leaves the
    room), raise it for other-epoch heartbeats too (both the first and the second), and refuse `at <=` it. For a
    newcomer nothing local helps; either accept and document it, or refuse other-epoch heartbeats until one for my own
    epoch from that leaf was taken (then "newer than" has a baseline).

Q2. 1c forgeability and replay.

   47-59); `from` must be the lowest reachable id below me (groupMerge.ts:79).

   twice, wrong order, two within 8 s of signed time or of my clock: refused (PR tests, read; R1/N1 use >= 8 s).
 - From a previous call / another Talk: no. The signed bytes hold the group id (derived from the channel id), and a
   MediaGroup makes a new leaf key per open, so a heartbeat from another Talk or from before the lead's reload does
   not verify. From a previous EPOCH of the same lead tab: yes, that is B3 (the leaf signature key is stable).
 - "Increasing time" is the SENDER's claimed time (lastBeatAt, groupEpoch.ts:216-218), never compared with my clock;
   my clock only enforces the 8 s between first and second. An honest lead with a wrong or jumping clock cannot

   re-run: one wrong-epoch heartbeat every 9 s for 600 s -> Carol starts over 11 times (29, 47, 74, 119, 182, then
   every 63 s). Bound: resets backoff. It is the committer and can do the same with a commit ahead at main; no gain.

Q3. 1b flapping, the per-sender limit, package freshness.
 - Two honest groups pulling members back and forth: no. A welcome replaces a group only from an id below my group's

 - Once per minute per sender across restarts: `rivals` is a field of the MediaGroup and is not reset by discard(),
   so it holds across start-overs of the group; it does not hold across a page reload (a new MediaGroup), as before.
   It is spent on welcomes that were not built on my package (B2), which hurts the honest case, not safety.
 - Freshness: after a join the package is replaced (renewOwn), after a start-over too (rekey), and an offer that

   Not fresh: the package of a member that FOUNDED and never joined (B4); its merge offer and its group-of-one
   announcement are that founding package, and a welcome built on it that was never taken stays valid.

 - Split room of 12 (6+6), production timing, honest relay: one member merges per round and the rounds double
   (B2): about 25 min until one group (first reviewer's run, 1527 s; 6 key packages, 6 welcomes, 6 commits). Per member
   the offer rate is at most one key package per tick (30 s) and none while the rival wait runs, so the handshake rate

 - With a lead that does not answer the rate is not bounded by 1b itself (B1): room of 12, one hour: 873 key packages,
   445 welcomes, 446 commits (first reviewer's run on this head); my room of 3: 171 / 82 / 83; my room of 4 with a

   `elsewhere` are plain fields. `elsewhere` is not cleared by discard(); harmless, it compares the state object.
 - Reset backoff: 1c honours it (waitMs check, then recoverNow -> allow). When the backoff blocks, the first heartbeat
   stays armed and the next one after the wait fires. Swap backoff: is what throttles B1's swaps (3 s doubling to
   60 s per member), and is the reason B1 settles at "one swap per member per minute" instead of per tick.
 - Mixed rooms: an old lowest id adds whoever offers (fine). An OLD committer of the higher group treats a new
   client's merge offer as a reload and swaps it, like a new one does (B1 mechanics, by reading; the code path
   receiveReturningMember is unchanged from main). Old members of a group without the lead stay behind as the PR says.
